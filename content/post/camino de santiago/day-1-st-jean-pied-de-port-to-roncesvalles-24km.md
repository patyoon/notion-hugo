I could not easily fall asleep, partly from excitement and partly because of the snoring in the room. The pilgrim’s office had warned me about the heatwave, so most of the people in the room had already left when I woke up at 6. I got a cup of coffee and started walking. The trail was already full of people.

{{< image classes="fancybox clear fig-75" group="1" src="/uploads/img_7409-large_fotor.jpg" >}}

{{< image classes="fancybox clear fig-75" group="1" src="/uploads/snapseed-2-_fotor.jpg" title="The high route over the Pyrenees is called Route Napoleon even though Napoleon never set foot on the path">}}

I met my Camino buddies in the first two hours of the most challenging section of the entire French Way: Peyman from Malmo, Sweden; Holly from Brighton, UK; and Anne from Nurnberg, Germany.

{{< wide-image src="/uploads/img_8858-large_fotor.jpg" >}}

We had met only a few hours after starting the walk, but we could already feel a strong bond forming as we conquered the mountain and savored the breathtaking views.

{{< youtube_shorts "https://www.youtube.com/embed/LoBcUpz5MQE" >}}

{{< wide-image src="/uploads/img_7459-large_fotor.jpg" >}}

The day was mostly uphill, with hikers, animals, and bicycles sharing the path.

{{< youtube_shorts "https://www.youtube.com/embed/UDlwlvAVfAo" >}}

When we reached the top of the Pyrenees and crossed the border to Spain, the blazing sun was directly above us, and there was no shade to hide under.

{{< youtube_shorts "https://www.youtube.com/embed/cWaCA4wGC1w" >}}

After the steep walk down to Roncesvalles, it was so hot that I broke my record for chugging a can of Diet Coke.

I could barely climb into the bunk bed and stopped moving for an hour. After dinner at the Albergue, I attended Mass. I could not understand anything, but I could feel the energy and spirit in the cathedral.
